# Character Creation — Scenario-Based Questions

**System:** 8 formative moments from your past. For each, you choose how you responded. Your choices shape your starting personality trait.

---

## Scenario 1: Father's Daily Command

**The moment:**
Your father needed wood cut. Every morning, before dawn: "Cut the wood." You cut it. Stacked it. For weeks, the same task, the same command. One morning he appears, ready to give the order again—even though you're already heading out to do it. What did you do?

**A) Say nothing, do it**
> You don't answer. You know what needs doing. You go, you cut, you stack. The work has its own weight; words only add noise.

**Effects:**
- Conscientiousness +2
- Extraversion -1

---

**B) Speak carefully**
> "Father, I know. I did it yesterday, and the day before." You don't challenge him, but you show him you've understood. Maybe he'll trust you next time.

**Effects:**
- Conscientiousness +1
- Extraversion +1
- Agreeableness +1

---

**C) Push back**
> "Enough." You say it quietly, but you say it. You have eyes. You have hands. You have a mind. He doesn't need to tell you what you already see.

**Effects:**
- Extraversion +2
- Openness +1
- Agreeableness -2

---

**D) Disappear before he speaks**
> You leave before he wakes. You cut the wood in the dark, before dawn. That way, there's no command to receive. No command, no conflict.

**Effects:**
- Openness +2
- Extraversion -1
- Neuroticism +1

---

## Scenario 2: The Dishonest Sale

**The moment:**
You worked a market stall. A customer came—ordinary person, no wealth to spare. Your employer nodded at the flour on the back shelf. It was old. Not spoiled, but aging. He wanted you to sell it as fresh. The customer waited. What did you do?

**A) You sold it**
> Everyone does it. The flour still works. The price is lower. You took the coin and wrapped it. The work is the work.

**Effects:**
- Conscientiousness -1
- Agreeableness -2
- Extraversion -1

---

**B) You hinted at the truth**
> "This flour is from last month," you said quietly. "It's solid, but it's not fresh. Cheaper price, if that matters to you."

**Effects:**
- Agreeableness +2
- Conscientiousness +1

---

**C) You refused**
> "I'm not selling that," you said. To the customer, to your employer. You walked to the other shelf and brought down the good flour. "This is what I'll sell."

**Effects:**
- Agreeableness +2
- Conscientiousness +2
- Extraversion +1

---

**D) You sold it—and adjusted**
> You sold the flour. But you weighted the scale a little in their favor. They got slightly more than they paid for. You kept the peace and balanced it your own way.

**Effects:**
- Agreeableness +1
- Conscientiousness -1
- Openness +1

## Scenario 3: The Bigger Child

**The moment:**
You were playing in the dirt. Another child came. Older. Larger. "This is my place now," they said. You both held stones. They waited for a challenge. What did you do?

**A) You left**
> There are other places to play. No reason to fight. You picked up your things and found somewhere else. Simple.

**Effects:**
- Conscientiousness +1
- Agreeableness +1
- Extraversion -1
- Neuroticism +1

---

**B) You talked**
> "We could both play here," you said. "Stones, or building, or whatever you want. There's room."

**Effects:**
- Agreeableness +2
- Extraversion +2

---

**C) You threw first**
> No words. Action. You threw your stone—hard, aimed, no hesitation. Let them see you meant it.

**Effects:**
- Extraversion +2
- Openness +1
- Conscientiousness -1

---

**D) You chose your ground**
> You stayed, but you moved—not away, but to better footing. You weighed your stone in your hand and looked at them calmly. "I'm staying. You decide what happens next."

**Effects:**
- Openness +2
- Conscientiousness +1
- Extraversion +1

## Scenario 4: The Landlord's Mistake

**The moment:**
The man who owned your room called you in. He named a rent you owed. But you knew—the number was wrong. He'd already taken payment this month. You knew because you kept track. He was larger than you, older, in his own hall. What did you do?

**A) You paid it**
> Not worth the argument. You couldn't afford to lose housing. You paid, said thank you, and left.

**Effects:**
- Conscientiousness -1
- Agreeableness +1
- Extraversion -1
- Neuroticism +1

---

**B) You asked politely**
> "Forgive me, but last week you took payment. I have the record here. Could we check?"

**Effects:**
- Extraversion +1
- Agreeableness +1
- Conscientiousness +1

---

**C) You told him plainly**
> "That's not the amount owed. Last week, I paid this month's rent. Here's my record. We can settle it now."

**Effects:**
- Extraversion +2
- Conscientiousness +2
- Agreeableness -1

---

**D) You brought the proof first**
> You said nothing until you had your notes in order. You brought them—every date, every coin. You laid them on his table and waited. Words after paper.

**Effects:**
- Conscientiousness +3
- Openness +1
- Extraversion -1

## Scenario 5: The Injured Stranger

**The moment:**
You found a man on the road. Wounded. Unconscious. A satchel lay near him—you could see silver inside. No one was watching. What did you do?

**A) You left him**
> Not your concern. You took the satchel—he wouldn't miss it—and moved on.

**Effects:**
- Agreeableness -3
- Conscientiousness -1

---

**B) You got help**
> You went to the nearest town. "There's a man down on the north road," you told someone with authority. "He needs a healer." You didn't go yourself, but you made sure someone would.

**Effects:**
- Agreeableness +1
- Conscientiousness +1
- Extraversion +1

---

**C) You stayed and helped**
> You tore cloth for a bandage. Gave him water when he woke. You stayed until you could get him to safety.

**Effects:**
- Agreeableness +3
- Conscientiousness +2

---

**D) You helped—and kept the satchel**
> You stayed with him. Gave him water. Got him to town. But when you found his satchel, you kept it. He survived; you needed the coin. Balanced enough.

**Effects:**
- Agreeableness +1
- Conscientiousness -1
- Openness +1

## Scenario 6: The Master's Wrong Route

**The moment:**
You were learning a trade from a master craftsman. One day, he said to take the direct route through the mountains—it would save hours. But you knew that route. A storm had blocked it two weeks ago. The safe route takes longer. He's your superior. He's made this journey before. What did you do?

**A) You went his way**
> He's the master. He knows more than you do. You followed his direction and went the direct route. If it was wrong, that's his error, not yours.

**Effects:**
- Conscientiousness +1
- Agreeableness +2
- Extraversion -1
- Neuroticism +1

---

**B) You questioned carefully**
> "Master, I heard the direct route was blocked by a slide two weeks ago. Is that no longer a problem?"

**Effects:**
- Extraversion +1
- Conscientiousness +1
- Agreeableness +1

---

**C) You objected**
> "The direct route is blocked. We go the safe way." You were respectful, but you didn't move until he understood what you were saying.

**Effects:**
- Extraversion +2
- Conscientiousness +2
- Agreeableness -1

---

**D) You showed what you knew**
> "I came through two weeks ago. The slide is still there. I can show you another route—it's longer, but I've scouted it." You offered knowledge, not defiance.

**Effects:**
- Openness +2
- Conscientiousness +1
- Extraversion +1

## Scenario 7: The Sound in the Dark

**The moment:**
Night. You were alone in the forest, camped. A sound came through the trees—large, moving. Footsteps, maybe. Or something bigger. Your heart moved fast. You had no weapon, no fire to show. What did you do?

**A) You stayed still**
> You sat quiet. You listened. You waited for morning. Most things that hunt avoid a waking human.

**Effects:**
- Conscientiousness +1
- Openness +1
- Neuroticism -1

---

**B) You called out**
> "Who's there?" Your voice wasn't steady, but you needed to know. Silence sometimes kills faster than words.

**Effects:**
- Extraversion +1
- Neuroticism +1

---

**C) You ran**
> You packed as much as you could carry and moved away from the sound. Fear is a useful signal.

**Effects:**
- Extraversion +1
- Openness +1
- Neuroticism +2

---

**D) You prepared silently**
> You moved slowly, quietly, toward better ground. You found a tree, climbed it high enough to see. You waited and watched.

**Effects:**
- Conscientiousness +2
- Openness +1
- Neuroticism -1

## Scenario 8: The Same Trick Again

**The moment:**
Weeks ago, you did something small. Dishonest, maybe. Risky, maybe. No one caught you. No one was harmed, not seriously. Now you have the chance to do it again—better this time, you think. You know the risk is small. You know you could get away with it. The benefit is real. What did you do?

**A) You did it again**
> You learned from before. You're more careful now. The first time worked; this time will be smoother.

**Effects:**
- Openness +2
- Extraversion +1
- Conscientiousness -2
- Agreeableness -1

---

**B) You considered it, then walked away**
> You could. You might. But each time you do it, the risk changes. You know that now. You let it go.

**Effects:**
- Conscientiousness +2
- Agreeableness +1
- Neuroticism -1

---

**C) You told someone**
> You found someone you trusted. You told them what you did. You didn't ask permission or forgiveness—you needed to speak it aloud to someone real.

**Effects:**
- Agreeableness +2
- Extraversion +1
- Conscientiousness +1

---

**D) You changed the method**
> You wanted the benefit, but not the lie. You found a way to get what you needed honestly. It took longer, but it was yours.

**Effects:**
- Agreeableness +2
- Conscientiousness +2
- Openness +1

## Scoring System

Each answer awards points to the Five Big traits:
- **C** = Conscientiousness (discipline, order, planning)
- **E** = Extraversion (courage, directness, social ease)
- **A** = Agreeableness (honesty, kindness, mercy)
- **O** = Openness (curiosity, adaptability, unorthodoxy)
- **N** = Neuroticism (reversed: Stability = calm, control)

After 8 scenarios (3-4 choices each):
1. Sum the points for each trait
2. Find the **highest total**
3. Award that trait as the starting personality
4. Grant the companion trait to the player

**Example:**
```
Conscientiousness: 14 points
Extraversion: 18 points ← HIGHEST
Agreeableness: 12 points
Openness: 10 points
Neuroticism (Stability): 8 points

Result: "You are naturally bold and direct. 
Your starting trait is Proud."
```

## UI/UX Design

**Screen flow:**

1. **Title card:** "Who Were You Made By?" (full-screen text)
2. **Scenario card:**
   - Bold scenario title
   - Italicized story text (2-3 sentences)
   - 3-4 choice buttons below
3. **Choice buttons:** 
   - Each labeled A, B, C, D
   - Short descriptive text (1 sentence, player's voice)
   - Click to select and advance
4. **Progress:** "Your Story: 3/8" (bottom center)

**Styling:**
- Parchment background
- Medieval font (serif, warm)
- Button style: Wood frame, worn edges
- Text: Olive/brown on aged cream
- No score visible (scoring is silent)

**Tone:** Personal, intimate, no judgment. Every choice is valid. Every person is shaped differently.

---

## Notes

- **Voice:** First-person singular ("You did," not "The character did")
- **Pacing:** Each scenario ~30 seconds to read and choose
- **Total time:** ~4 minutes for all 8
- **No "right" answer:** All choices are paths, not failures
- **Result feels earned:** Player's choices directly shape their starting trait
